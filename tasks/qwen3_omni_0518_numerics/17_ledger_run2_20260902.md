## 4. Lazy graph capture inside serving, Qwen3-TTS

The Qwen3-TTS code predictor runs after the backbone forward, in
`post_decode` through `_collect_codes`
(`qwen3_tts/model_runner.py:83-90`, `:212-239`, the call at `:230`), and
replays a model owned CUDA graph keyed by `(batch bucket, sampling
signature)` (`qwen3_tts/sglang_model.py:1295`). That graph is captured
the first time a key is seen, inside the serving step
(`sglang_model.py:1297-1317`): two eager warmup passes of the predictor
chain on a side stream and a third pass under `torch.cuda.graph`
(`sglang_model.py:120-146`). Nothing captures it at startup. The bucket
ladder is sglang's decode ladder clipped to `max_running_requests`
(`sglang_model.py:1163-1187`), so at the 64 row launch of this run the
keys were 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64. Prefill shares the
cache (`model_runner.py:74-81`).

The server log dates every capture and the ledger shows what it cost.
The host wall below is the ledger's `host_ms` max of the (mode, rows)
shape that paid the capture, read from the step ledger JSON of each
window. An earlier version of this table read eight of the twelve off
the wrong rows and reported them as 14 to 166 ms, that was wrong.

| capture | log time | window | step that paid | host wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bucket 1 | 15:52:23 | c1 | extend rows 1, the first prefill, whose own forward was 232 ms | 1089 ms |
| bucket 16 | 15:52:55 | c16 | extend rows 15, 15 requests waiting | 915 ms |
| bucket 12 | 15:52:56 | c16 | decode rows 12 | 565 ms |
| bucket 8 | 15:52:57 | c16 | decode rows 8 | 531 ms |
| bucket 4 | 15:52:57 | c16 | decode rows 4 | 503 ms |
| bucket 2 | 15:52:58 | c16 | decode rows 2 | 545 ms |
| bucket 32 | 15:53:07 | c32 | decode rows 32 | 497 ms |
| bucket 24 | 15:53:08 | c32 | decode rows 24 | 523 ms |
| bucket 64 | 15:53:16 | c64 | decode rows 64 | 512 ms |
| bucket 56 | 15:53:17 | c64 | decode rows 56 | 513 ms |
| bucket 48 | 15:53:17 | c64 | decode rows 45 | 506 ms |
| bucket 40 | 15:53:18 | c64 | decode rows 38 | 496 ms |

Every capture costs about 500 ms, whatever the bucket and whatever the
allocator held. Two carry more: the first capture in the process, and
the one right after the 15 row prefill burst. Each capture step also
records about 4400 allocator allocations against about 50 for a normal
step (the c64 rows 56 step is a single step at 4420, the others are
derived from the per shape averages). The ledger counts tensor
allocations, so that figure is the chain run three times, two warmup
passes and the capture pass, at about 1470 allocations per pass.

The cost is therefore not the chain's device time, which is
milliseconds at every bucket. A cost that ignores the batch size and
scales with the number of passes is host work, and the working
hypothesis is three host passes over a chain of roughly 2500 PyTorch
ops (16 one token forwards through the 5 predictor layers plus 15
sampling groups, with the real checkpoint's config) plus the graph
instantiate, with the device synchronize and the allocator flush of
`torch.cuda.graph` as the variable part on top. That split is measured
by the capture phase timing on perf/step-ledger and the plan that
follows from it is doc 19. The two warmup passes also run the eager
branches of the fused sampler, gather and addmm, which are gated on
`is_current_stream_capturing()` (`sglang_model.py:1496`, `:1712`,
`:1820`), so the fused Triton kernels are launched for the first time
inside the capture, compile included.

Production traffic sees new buckets whenever concurrency crosses a rung,
and every request in the batch waits for the capture. The c16 window
paid it five times, the c64 window four times. CI hides it because its
warmup at C touches the buckets at and below C.

The talker's predictor graph has the same lazy path in the code
(`qwen3_omni/components/talker.py:1505-1535`, logged at `:1530`) but the
serve log shows exactly one capture, at boot for batch size 32 (15:48:02),
and none inside any window, so its 234 ms first 4 row prefill is not a
predictor capture. Neither that step nor MOSS-TTS Delay's 198 ms first 2
row prefill has a named cause in the omni code that was read (the delay
sampler graph is captured at engine init). Both are one off, first shape
costs of about 200 ms and stay open as task T26.

## 5. The post forward phase, per runner

### 5.1 Qwen3-TTS: the predictor chain is the step

One decode step is the backbone graph replay (2.07 ms), an eager
semantic sampler, the predictor graph replay, and the staged token copy
(`base.py:238-297`, `model_runner.py:104`, `:212-239`). Inside the
predictor graph the chain is `num_code_groups` single token forwards
over the predictor's layers, each sub code sampled and fed to the next
(`sglang_model.py:1369-1420`), strictly serial by data dependency and
fully batched over rows. That is the 5.6 ms after the forward at one row
and the 6.2 to 7.2 ms at 16 to 64 rows: a latency bound chain whose
cost is kernel count, not rows. The host waits 6.5 to 8.3 ms on the
staged token event, so the device is the constraint.

Two consequences. The step is 7.7 to 11 ms for 1 to 64 rows, so
throughput is rows per step and the engine is not the limit at c32 or
c64 (section 6). And the c1 latency lever is inside the predictor chain:
fewer kernels per sub step, or the two graphs and the sampler between
them fused into one launch.

The prefill has the same shape: a one token cache hit costs the same as
a 54 token cold prefill (forward 11.45 against 11.55, host 18.05 against
18.23), because the prefill step runs the same predictor chain on its
last position (`model_runner.py:74-81`).

### 5.2 Qwen3-Omni talker: the forward is the whole model, the rest is shared

The talker has no custom decode forward. Its sglang decode graph holds
the 20 MoE layers, the codec head, the sampler and all 16 code predictor
sub steps (`qwen3_omni/components/talker.py:1275-1294`, the predictor at
`:1291-1294`, 16 single token passes over 5 dense layers each). So
`forward_ms` is the entire model: 5.27 ms at one row, 6.1 to 7.8 ms at
6 to 8 rows. That is the talker's true per row growth, about 0.3 ms per
row, and the doc 16 reading of 1.3 ms per row was the span, not the
model.

What the span holds outside the forward, with no host to device
synchronization after the forward at all (`talker_model_runner.py:105-121`,
`:133-154`, `base.py:284-291`):

- before the forward: the FutureMap input resolve and the forward batch
  build, then `prepare_decode_buffers`. Its fast path
  (`talker.py:1029-1049`) is voided by every prefill (`:1051-1054`) and
  by any change of the row set, and the slow path walks a 1023 entry
  suppress list per row in Python (`talker.py:1113-1122`) and waits on
  the previous staging copy (`:1127`) before uploading. At c16 and c32
  the row set changes every few steps, so the slow path is the common
  path
- after the forward: one clone of the sampled ids, the staged pinned
  copy, two batched clones, and one outbox message per row
  (`talker_model_runner.py:145-153`). The outbox is drained on the stage's
  asyncio thread in the same process, which exports a CUDA IPC handle
  and sends over ZMQ per message (`pipeline/stage/runtime.py:1507-1519`,
  `comm/stage_io.py:776-781`), so rows messages per step compete for the
  GIL with the step loop
- device time that is not the talker's: code2wav decodes on the same GPU
  inside the talker's window, and its per request tail decode runs eager
  for 60 to 108 ms (section 6), as does the thinker. The thinker's own
  forward, a pure graph replay, costs 5.99 ms at one row at c16 against
  4.33 at c1, so device sharing alone adds about 40 percent to any span
  in this topology

The 4.5 to 5.4 ms of span after the forward at c16 rows 6 to 8, and the
1.9 to 2.1 ms at c32 rows 6 to 7, are these three together, and one GPU
cannot apportion them. The host side is visible on its own: host minus
wait is 5.6 ms per step at c16 rows 7 against 0.9 ms at c1, and the
device idle floor is 2.2 ms at rows 7 against 0.3 at c1, which is the
pre forward host work delaying the launch. Task T23 splits the topology
(talker on its own GPU) to read the talker's own span, and measures the
slow path of `prepare_decode_buffers` directly.

The readiness gate (`talker_scheduler.py:96-137`) is host only, but a
miss undoes an already prepared decode with three device subtractions
and an indexed write outside any ledger span, and the miss probability
rises with rows because readiness is an all over rows.

### 5.3 MOSS-TTS-Local and MOSS-TTS Delay: a stream drain inside every step

Neither runner overrides the decode forward, so the ledger's forward
bracket holds the backbone graph replay alone (`moss_tts_local/sglang_model.py:305-329`,
`moss_tts/sglang_model.py:296-320`, both keep heads and sampling out of
the forward on purpose). Everything model specific runs in `post_decode`.

MOSS-TTS-Local, per decode step:

- before the forward, a Python loop over rows builds the pool row list and
  uploads it with `torch.tensor(list, device=cuda)`
  (`moss_tts_local/state_pool.py:279-287`)
- the backbone graph replay, 3.74 ms at one row
- the frame decode as one CUDA graph replay per batch bucket, captured at
  engine init (`moss_tts_local/sglang_model.py:451-522`, replay at
  `:573`): 12 local transformer micro steps and 13 seeded sampling passes.
  The code's own note says the eager form is about 500 kernels and 22 ms
  per frame (`sglang_model.py:455-456`)
- the GPU radix row hash, a 13 channel unrolled loop of four launches per
  channel (`moss_tts_local/radix_hash.py:65-68`), about 55 launches and
  as many allocations, which is most of the 94 to 100 allocations per
  step
- a second `torch.tensor(list, device=cuda)` for the emit indices after
  the frame decode (`moss_tts_local/model_runner.py:418`), then four
  index selects and two index puts (`:422-446`)
- after the span, the blocking token copy in the output processor
  (`output_processor.py:38`): this runner never stages tokens, so the
  ledger's end event wait at `base.py:568` finds the device already done

A `torch.tensor(list, device=cuda)` from pageable memory synchronizes the
stream before it copies, so the step drains the device twice: once before
the forward and once right after the frame decode graph. That is why host
equals span with wait zero on this runner, and why the ledger cannot
split the 2.8 ms after the forward between the frame graph, the hash
launches and host starvation. What the numbers do say: the backbone is
55 percent of the step, the frame graph plus the hash chain is the rest,
flat in rows, device side.

MOSS-TTS Delay, per decode step:

- before the forward, a per row deque pop and a stack (`moss_tts/model_runner.py:147-159`)
- the backbone graph replay, 6.12 ms at one row
- 33 eager LM head calls, one per channel, each through sglang's
  `LogitsProcessor` with a float32 cast (`moss_tts/sglang_model.py:348-364`,
  the cast at sglang `logits_processor.py:888`), 66 to 99 launches
- on the graphed path (all rows audio, default sampling profile,
  `model_runner.py:206-227`): two more `torch.tensor(list, device=cuda)`
  uploads of seeds and generation steps (`model_runner.py:240-249`), then
  the delay FSM and sampler as one CUDA graph replay
  (`moss_tts/sampling_cuda_graph.py:327`)
- 33 eager embedding lookups and adds for the feedback
  (`moss_tts/sglang_model.py:259-267`)
- after the span, the blocking token copy, then a per row loop that
  clones two device tensors per row per step (`model_runner.py:792-794`)
- off the graphed path (any text row, or any non default sampling
  parameter) the sampler runs eager with nine list uploads and about
  fifteen `.any()`, `nonzero` and mask index syncs per step
  (`model_runner.py:358-486`)

The two uploads at `:240-249` drain the stream after the 33 heads on
every graphed step, the same signature as Local. The 190 to 420
allocations per step are the 33 heads and 33 embeddings. The post
forward part is 1.9 ms and flat in rows, and the per row clone loop
after the span grows with rows on the host.

Both runners have the same omni owned fix available: keep the per step
row metadata on the device or in a pinned buffer instead of rebuilding a
Python list and uploading it inside the step, and fuse the unrolled hash
and the 33 way head and embedding fans into one launch each. The sync
contract test of doc 14 (T2) is what proves the drain is gone.

### 5.4 Qwen3-ASR prefill: the gate admits every batch, 39 percent still ran eager

Cold prefills of 67 tokens at c1 split 61 percent replaying the prefill
graph at 4.1 ms of forward and 39 percent eager at 8.7 ms of forward and
9.1 ms of host. The read of the whole path finds no per request property
that can select eager:

- the audio reaches the forward only as a precomputed embedding on
  `ForwardBatch.mm_inputs` (`qwen3_asr/request_builders.py:296-324`,
  `:362`, `encoder_service.py:360-370`, sglang `forward_batch_info.py:799`).
  `input_embeds` and `replace_embeds` stay None (the `Req` at
  `request_builders.py:354-363`, sglang `forward_batch_info.py:781-782`), so
  the two embedding clauses of `can_replay_locally` cannot fire
- every other clause is constant for this deployment (breakable backend,
  no LoRA, no speculative decode, no logprobs, no hidden capture, no DP),
  and the padding rule with the 4 to 4096 ladder rejects only one token
  extends
- the encoder runs ahead of the step on its own thread and stream
  (`encoder_service.py:124-128`, `:405-415`) and a request is admitted
  only once its embedding is attached (`request_builders.py:376-390`,
  `omni_scheduler.py:1106-1156`), so the step never waits for it

The ledger's graph flag is a straight pass through of sglang's
`can_run_graph` (`base.py:596`, `model_worker.py:295-299`). What the eager
steps cost is explained: both paths run the same outer forward, and only
the 28 layer body differs, one replay against about ten launches per
layer, whose host launch gaps land inside `forward_ms`. Why the gate said
no for 39 percent is not explained by the code and is task T25. The
branch records, since 5a0a6a6e2, the token count and batch size of every
eager prefill in the worker's graph usage info (`model_worker.py`, under
`prefill_cuda_graph.standard_eager_shapes` in `/model_info`), so the next
run answers it from the server rather than from a guess.

The request build (5.6 ms p50 at c1) runs on the eight worker build pool,
not the scheduler thread (`omni_scheduler.py:894`, `:932-945`): audio
decode, resample and fingerprint (`preprocessing/transcription.py:64-95`),
the Whisper mel extraction padded to the clip's length
(`request_builders.py:257-266`), the prompt and the multimodal item, and
either a blocking wait for the encoder or a deferred admission
(`:376-390`). MOSS-TD's 16.9 ms build at c1 has the same shape.

## 6. Smaller findings

- **A fully cached prompt costs more than a cold one on Higgs and
  Qwen3-ASR.** The one token extend runs eager under sglang's padding rule
  (doc 16 section 1): Higgs 12.55 ms host and 11.88 forward for a hit
  against 8.94 and 7.64 for a 144 token cold prefill, Qwen3-ASR 11.68 and
  11.14 against 4.53 and 4.14 for 67 tokens. MOSS-TD does not pay it
  because its engine builder captures buckets 1 and 2
  (`moss_transcribe_diarize/engine_builder.py:106-112`). The same floor
  on the Higgs and Qwen3-ASR builders is task T19.
- **Qwen3-TTS is vocoder bound past c32.** QPS 22.25 at c32 and 22.22 at
  c64 while the engine's step stays at 10 to 11 ms and its mean batch at
  c64 is 33 rows (172 steps for 114 requests of 50 frames). The vocoder
  stage p50 per request is 72.6 ms at c1, 93.8 at c16, 157.7 at c32,
  306.3 at c64 (p95 869), on the 0.10 GPU fraction it gets. The engine
  has room, the vocoder does not.
- **code2wav's last window runs eager.** Four graphs exist for batch 1 at
  10, 20, 30 and 35 frames (serve log 15:47:52). Windowed decodes replay
  in 0.35 to 0.79 ms p50, and the final `stream_done` decode of every
  request, at 11 to 31 frames, runs eager in 60 to 108 ms. The code2wav
  stage p50 per request is 12 ms at c1 and 25 ms at c16, most of it that
  tail.
- **The talker to code2wav chunk hop is fine on the p50.** 0.17 ms at c1,
  0.31 at c16, 0.34 at c32. The c16 mean of 19.6 ms comes from 98 chunks
  over 100 ms during one 603 ms stall in code2wav, the largest first use
  cost in that window.
- **Qwen3-ASR at c8 and c32.** Queue wait p50 7.4 ms at c8 and 14.4 ms at
  c32, p95 at the 40 ms deadline in both, unchanged from run 1. The
  request build p50 is 5.6 ms at c1, 2.5 at c8, 3.6 at c32, against a 4.5
  ms prefill step.
- **MOSS-TD's request build** p50 16.9 ms at c1 against a 4.6 ms prefill
  step, 5.4 ms at c16. Run 1's mean was 4.9 ms at c1. The c1 build is on
  the cold speaker free ASR path, so the cache does not explain it, and
  the two runs were on different GPUs of a shared box. Task T17 measures
  it in isolation.
- **Higgs is clean.** 86 percent of its step is the backbone graph at one
  row and 95 percent at 16, its real prefill replays the prefill graph
  (0.98), and its vocoder p50 is 4.8 ms. Its throughput is rows per 4.5
  ms step. The only items are the cache hit path above and the codec
  frame cap of doc 16.

## 8. MiniMax Music 3, from the later window on the same commit

Tarball `minimax-step-ledger-7b1bee5c4.tar.gz`: one profiled window over
the cookbook helper's two passes (five songs one at a time, then the same

### 8.1 The AR step, now split

| shape | steps | cycle | host | wait | span | forward | after the forward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decode rows 2 (one song, guidance doubles rows) | 3454 | 12.32 | 12.11 | 0.00 | 11.99 | 6.61 | 5.38 |
| decode rows 10 (five songs) | 738 | 13.37 | 13.11 | 0.00 | 12.98 | 7.21 | 5.77 |
| extend rows 2, 200 tokens | 6 | 27.46 | 26.89 | 0.00 | 26.58 | 20.55 | |
| extend rows 8, 822 tokens | 1 | 236.16 | 235.37 | 0.00 | 235.01 | 35.30 | |

The backbone graph is 6.6 ms at two rows and 7.2 ms at ten, memory bound
scaling as expected. The 5.4 to 5.8 ms after it is flat in rows and is
the runner's own post decode: an explicit stream synchronize before the
chunk is staged (`minimax_music3/model_runner.py:406`) and a `tolist()` of
the stop flags (`:277`) inside the step, so host equals span with wait
zero by construction, as doc 16 predicted. That is 44 percent of every AR
step, and the staged token event of the talker is the pattern that
removes it (T27). The single 8 row prefill of the parallel pass cost 235
ms of host for a 35 ms forward, the first shape cost of T26 again.

The AR batches well: five songs at ten rows take 738 steps of 13.4 ms,
9.9 s for all five, against 9.4 s each alone.

### 8.2 The acoustic stage is the limit at any concurrency above one

The request view over both passes:

| interval | p50 |
|---|---|
| AR stage per song | 9.81 s |
| AR prefill | 116 ms |
| prefill start to first chunk emitted | 2.78 s |
| AR to DiT chunk hop | 57.7 ms, payload hop 74.6 ms, across GPUs |
| DiT stage complete after its last chunk, one song at a time | 1.1 to 1.6 s |
| DiT stage complete after its last chunk, five at once | 27.5 to 31.2 s |

The acoustic scheduler decodes every chunk as it arrives, serially
(`minimax_music3/acoustic.py:327-336`, `max_batch_size=1`, one
`decode_with_state` per chunk at `:351-415`), and each chunk runs 30 DiT
steps at CFG 1.7 in float32 through the compiled blocks, then the DAV
decoder, then a host copy (`stages.py:83-88`, `constants.py:12-13`,
`acoustic.py:269-311`). Chunks arrive every 1.23 s at one song and one
chunk takes about 1.1 s to decode, so at one song the stage keeps up and
finishes 1.4 s after the AR. At five songs 35 chunks arrive in the same
10 s and the serial stage needs about 38 s for them, which is the 27 to
31 s tail after the AR ends. The wall for five songs is 41.6 s against
50.8 s one at a time, a 1.22x gain from 5x concurrency, and all of the
missing gain is this stage.

Its knobs already exist as factory arguments (`stages.py:78-96`: dtype,
`dit_steps`, `cache_dit`, `compile_acoustic`, `breakable_cuda_graph`), and
its batch dimension is one. T28 is the A/B over those knobs at five
songs, then batching chunks across streams in the acoustic scheduler.

### 8.3 Launch

Four detached launches (`setsid`, backgrounded, output redirected) died
with exit code -9 inside the dit_dav process during scheduler
construction, on three different GPU pairs, with no OOM record. A plain
foreground launch on the same pair succeeded. No omni code sets a
parent death signal or kills a process group (grep over `pipeline`,
`serve`, `cli`), so the kill comes from the launch environment. Until it
is understood, launch MiniMax in the foreground under a terminal
multiplexer.
